I am a PhD student in Geography and the Environment at The University of Texas at Austin, advised by Kelley A. Crews. I build scale-aware GeoAI that estimates environmental exposure (urban heat, air quality, land change) from Earth observation, then test whether the answer holds: when the map unit changes, against ground measurements, and against what people experience. I release the code and data behind this work so others can check and reuse it.

### Research themes

- **Scale-aware exposure assessment:** how map units (MAUP) and spatial uncertainty change who looks exposed
- **Urban heat and climate resilience:** land surface temperature, heat islands and cooling strategies from satellites and lidar
- **Field-validated spatial machine learning:** satellite estimates calibrated and tested against ground observations

### Featured

**[GeoXVIEW](https://github.com/AbdullaKafy/GeoXVIEW)**: dissertation framework for scale-adaptive environmental exposure in Texas. Five layers from satellite to street level, field-calibrated bias correction, MAUP diagnostics across five map units, Python package and a [live dashboard](https://abdullakafy.github.io/GeoXVIEW/). Field calibration cut the satellite-to-ground temperature gap from 20.7 °F to about ±3 °F.

**[landsat-climate-ml](https://github.com/AbdullaKafy/landsat-climate-ml)**: an open, tested Python toolkit for land cover change, LST, UHI, UTFVI, CA-ANN prediction, carbon stock and drought from Landsat. Eleven published studies (about 1,180 citations) each get a page with the published tables as data, a key figure and runnable sample code.

**[green-roof-cooling-lidar](https://github.com/AbdullaKafy/green-roof-cooling-lidar)**: lidar urban form and neural networks to test green roof cooling, with a worked example on open USGS 3DEP lidar for downtown Austin: greening 4.2% of roof area lowers predicted daytime surface temperature by about 2.7 °F.

**[publications-code](https://github.com/AbdullaKafy/publications-code)**: every journal article with DOI, citations and a link to its code where one exists.
